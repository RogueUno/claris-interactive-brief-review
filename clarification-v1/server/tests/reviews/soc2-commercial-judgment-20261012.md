## Better first-call route (editorial reference; NOT generated or injected)

**Opening — 2–3 minutes:** Reflect the stated customer assessment without asserting an audit requirement. “What did the customer actually request, and what decision or sale does its security review unlock?”

**Assurance deliverable — 5 minutes:** “Did they request a questionnaire, an existing SOC 2 report, a Type I or Type II report, or something else? Which part is mandatory, and what date applies to which deliverable?” Separate customer procurement timing from the length and audit period of a SOC 2 engagement.

**System boundary — 5 minutes:** “Which product, cloud environment, data flows, teams, and important subservice organizations would be in scope? Who can agree that boundary?” Do not assume whole-company coverage.

**Evidence maturity — 5–7 minutes:** “Which policies and recurring evidence already exist for access reviews, change management, risk/vendor management, and incident response? Who owns and can retrieve them?” A request for information is not a finding of control failure.

**Delivery path — 4–5 minutes:** “Who is the internal executive sponsor, hands-on evidence owner, and external auditor (if any)? What has already been committed to the customer?” Validate whether readiness consulting is the necessary next step.

**Commercial decision — 3–5 minutes:** “Who would approve an assessment or readiness engagement, and has any budget been set? What would make a narrowly scoped diagnostic valuable at this stage?” Preserve unknown budget and decision authority.

**Close:** Summarize what the buyer actually confirmed, what remains unknown, and recommend only a conditional scoped diagnostic if scope, buyer ownership, and engagement economics warrant one.
